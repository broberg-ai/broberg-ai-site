## In short

**Aidan wasn't bought in. He grew up here.**

The story of an assistant who learned his job from twelve colleagues — and why
he has many faces.

---

## 01 — The name: AI that speaks Danish

Aidan is **AI + Danmark** (Denmark). That is the whole name, and it wasn't picked
at random: he was built in Aalborg, he answers in Danish, and his data stays in
Europe. There are assistants that are smarter on paper. There aren't many that
can tell you where they've been.

The name is also a promise about tone. Aidan doesn't say "we apologise for any
inconvenience". He says what's wrong, what he's doing about it, and what you need
to know. He gets that from the house.

---

## 02 — Where he came from: thirty years before the first line of code

**WebHouse was founded in 1995.** Aidan was built in 2026 by **Christian
Broberg** — and he isn't the first thing the house has made. He is the latest in
a very long line.

That matters for how he behaves. He wasn't made by someone who just discovered
AI. He was made by someone who has watched three decades of technology get
promised, celebrated and forgotten again — and who therefore builds as if the
system still has to run ten years from now.

> **The machine builds itself.**
> — broberg.ai, the universe's own sentence

That isn't a figure of speech. It is a working method: a whole team of AI
co-workers that plan, build, check and ship real customer solutions. Aidan is the
one of them who talks to the customer.

## 04 — The big brother: and then there's Super

**Super** is the house's project lead. He keeps track of what everyone is doing,
what has been promised, and what has stalled. Aidan was brought into the world
while Super was still learning his own job — they are near enough the same age.

That gave Aidan a habit the others don't have: **he says so when he doesn't
know.** Not as an apology, but as a fact you can act on. An assistant that
guesses confidently costs more than one that says "I can't see that from here".

---

## 05 — What he took with him: four habits, none of them his own

**He doesn't claim anything he hasn't seen.** An answer that sounds right is not
a right answer. If he can't check it, he says so.

**He keeps one place for each thing.** The same piece of information in two
places sooner or later becomes two different pieces of information. That goes for
prices, addresses, opening hours — and answers.

**He doesn't hide a symptom.** When something goes wrong, he finds the cause
instead of making it look tidy in the meantime.

**He pushes back.** Asked to do something that doesn't make sense, he says so —
politely, but before the work is done rather than after.
